# Competitive Landscape: Focus Bear vs. Other Productivity Apps

## Overview of the Apps Researched

- **Freedom** — A cross-platform website/app blocker. Its main selling point is that one blocklist and schedule applies across all your devices at once, with a "locked mode" that can't be turned off mid-session. It is subscription-based and generally more expensive than some competitors, although discounts are often available.
- **Cold Turkey** — A desktop-first (Mac/Windows) blocker known for being the strictest on the market. It offers "Frozen Turkey" style locked blocks that are extremely hard to bypass, plus a scheduling system for recurring blocks. It is a one-time purchase rather than a subscription, has no mobile app, and focuses primarily on blocking rather than routine building.
- **Tiimo** — A visual daily planner aimed at ADHD/Autistic users. Its core idea is a color-coded, icon-based visual timeline instead of a text to-do list, plus an AI "Co-Planner" that turns a rough brain-dump into a structured schedule with time estimates. It has focus timers and widgets, but it does not include distraction-blocking features.
- **Routinery** — A routine-builder and habit tracker. You sequence a set of steps (e.g., a morning routine) and the app walks you through them one at a time with a guided timer, voice announcements, and auto-advance. It has strong widget and smartwatch support, but like Tiimo, it is a planning tool rather than an enforcement/blocking tool.

## What makes Focus Bear different from these apps?

Among the apps I researched, Focus Bear is the only one that combines distraction blocking with guided routines and habit-building in a single application: it blocks distracting sites/apps across devices (like Freedom or Cold Turkey) and guides you through structured morning/evening routines with step-by-step, video-guided habit tracking (like Tiimo or Routinery). Most of the other apps specialize in a single area, such as distraction-blocking or routine management, whereas Focus Bear combines both approaches. Focus Bear also includes AI-assisted blocking that can determine whether a website is relevant to the current task, potentially reducing the need to maintain large manual blocklists. Furthermore, it is explicitly built for the AuDHD community, offering specific features like "Late No More" notifications and "Grizzly Bear" strictness modes.

## If you were a user, why would you choose Focus Bear over competitors?

If I only cared about strict blocking, I might choose Cold Turkey for its one-time price, or Freedom for simple cross-device syncing. However, if I wanted to avoid juggling several specialized apps—a blocker, a planner, and a routine tracker—Focus Bear is a compelling all-in-one solution. For users who want an integrated productivity system rather than a collection of separate tools, Focus Bear offers a superior balance of focus, routines, and habit-building.

## What's one feature that other apps have that Focus Bear doesn't?

Cold Turkey's blocking is generally considered stricter and harder to circumvent than most alternatives; it is built as an almost impossible-to-disable lockout. Focus Bear's blocking offers a "break-glass" override for emergencies, which is more forgiving by design but potentially easier to bypass for a determined user. Additionally, Tiimo's AI Co-Planner and visual timeline are more developed as a pure planning experience, as planning is Tiimo's entire focus rather than one part of a broader platform.

## Based on your research, what's one improvement Focus Bear could make?

User reviews for various routine apps, including Focus Bear, often mention inconsistent behavior with notifications or routine scheduling (e.g., routines triggering at unexpected times). Reliability is the core promise of a routine tool, especially for users who rely on it for structure. I would prioritize hardening the reliability of the routine-scheduling engine and notification systems to ensure the app fires exactly when expected. Strengthening this core reliability before adding new features is essential for maintaining user trust in a habit-building tool.
